# Known limitations

Written to be read by someone deciding whether to trust a number from `docs/results.md`.

## 1. The embeddings have no semantics

`embedding.embed` hashes character 3-grams and word unigrams into 128 signed columns. It is
not a sentence encoder and does not approximate one. `embed("the claim was approved")` and
`embed("the claim was denied")` are near-identical, because they share every 3-gram but
three.

Consequences, in descending order of importance:

- **Absolute detection delays are artefacts of the chosen effect sizes.** "Sliced PSI
  detects `retrieval-decay` on day 62" is a statement about this simulator. Do not carry
  the number to a production system.
- **The relative comparison is valid**, because all eight detectors read the same vectors
  and every threshold is calibrated by the same rule. Rankings, coverage, and the
  false-positive column are the results worth carrying.
- **One finding is representation-dependent and is flagged as such.** Self-similarity's
  response to `template-regression` is a *drop* in mean pairwise cosine from 0.3257 to
  0.3208 — the right direction for the right reason (a mixture of two tight clusters is
  less self-similar than one loose cluster), but a 1.5% effect. A semantic encoder would
  place boilerplate answers far closer together than hashed n-grams do, which should
  *strengthen* the effect; but the baseline self-similarity of unrelated answers would also
  change, and the sign is not guaranteed to survive. Re-derive it before quoting it.

## 2. Six scenarios are not a benchmark

Four regressions, one null, one benign shift, one seed. This is a demonstration of a method,
not a benchmark with confidence intervals. A different seed moves individual delays by a
day or two; the structural results (which detector covers which scenario, who fires on
`input-shift`) have been checked across seeds and are stable, but "checked across seeds"
means a handful, not a power analysis.

## 3. Everything is offline and single-tenant

There is no service, no queue, no retention policy and no multi-tenant isolation. Each day's
statistics are computed from that day's turns held in memory. A real deployment has to solve
sampling, storage, backfill, late-arriving data, and the question of what happens when the
monitoring pipeline itself falls behind — none of which appear here.

## 4. The reference window is assumed clean

Every threshold is calibrated from days 1–30, which the simulator guarantees are healthy. In
production nobody guarantees that. If the reference window contains a degradation, every
detector calibrates the degradation into "normal" and the panel goes quiet. This is the
single largest gap between this project and a deployable system, and the mitigation
(hold-out reference windows, periodic recalibration with human sign-off, drift-of-the-drift
monitoring) is not implemented.

## 5. The detectors are univariate-marginal or kernel-global

PSI reads 128 marginals independently and sums; it is blind to a change in the *correlation*
structure that leaves every marginal fixed. MMD sees the joint distribution but reduces it to
a single number, so it cannot say which direction moved. Neither localises a regression to a
cause. Sliced PSI localises to a topic, which is the coarsest useful granularity and was
enough here.

## 6. The quality score is simulated, not measured

`Turn.quality` is generated by the simulator, so "materiality" is defined by construction.
In production, quality is the hardest thing on the page: it is either a delayed human label,
a proxy (thumbs, escalation rate, handle time), or an LLM judge with its own drift. The
project deliberately treats the quality canary as *expensive but available*, which is
generous — in practice its 24 calls a day would also need a judge whose calibration is
itself monitored.

## 7. Alert semantics are first-crossing only

A detector "detects" on the first day its statistic exceeds threshold for `PERSISTENCE`
consecutive days. There is no de-duplication, no suppression, no severity, no recovery
detection, and no notion of an alert closing. Those are the parts of an alerting system that
determine whether anyone still reads it after a month.

## 8. `sliced_output_psi` needs traffic

Each slice needs `MIN_SLICE = 8` samples in both reference and current window or it is
skipped. With six topics and 12 topic-days of traffic per slice, the sliced detector sees
roughly a twelfth of the sample per test, which is exactly why it alerts 12 days later than
aggregate PSI on `model-swap`, the regression where dilution is not a problem and pooling
wins. On a low-traffic product the slices would
never fill and the detector would silently degrade to "no opinion" — silently, because a
skipped slice is not an alert. That failure mode is monitored by nothing here.
